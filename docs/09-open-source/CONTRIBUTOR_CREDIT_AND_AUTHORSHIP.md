# Contributor Credit, Research Authorship, and Public Recognition

## Principle

DRL treats credit as research infrastructure. Contribution is broader than code and includes design, review, evaluation, data work, documentation, accessibility, security, teaching, issue reproduction, community care, and independent replication.

## Credit surfaces

The laboratory maintains:

- repository contributor history;
- release-specific acknowledgments;
- `CITATION.cff` for citable projects;
- authors/contributors metadata in model and dataset cards;
- paper authorship and acknowledgments;
- website contributor directory with opt-in profiles;
- upstream and community replication ledgers;
- event and teaching credits;
- security acknowledgments when disclosure policy permits.

## Authorship criteria

Research authorship requires a substantial intellectual or technical contribution, participation in drafting or substantive review, approval of the public version, and accountability for the contribution. Project status, sponsorship, or title alone does not grant authorship. Contributions that do not meet authorship criteria receive visible acknowledgment.

## Release credit

Every release note identifies maintainers, authors, first-time contributors, evaluators, dataset reviewers, documentation and accessibility contributors, security reporters, and upstream work relied upon. Automated dependency lists do not replace human-readable credit.

## Consent and identity

Contributors choose whether public credit uses a legal name, preferred name, handle, or anonymous acknowledgment. DRL does not publish personal contact information without consent. Removal or correction requests are handled through document control while preserving necessary legal notices and Git history constraints.

## Anti-extraction rules

DRL will not:

- use unpaid take-home assignments as disguised product work;
- pressure learners to donate data or labor;
- promise future employment in exchange for contribution;
- erase upstream history when importing code;
- use community submissions to train a model without the stated consent and license path;
- convert a contributor's private trace into a public dataset without explicit review.

## Recognition without gamification

Badges may recognize completed mentorship, replication, review, or maintenance responsibilities, but leaderboards must not incentivize low-quality volume. The highest institutional recognition is demonstrated stewardship: improving the commons, helping others reproduce work, and maintaining reliable artifacts over time.
