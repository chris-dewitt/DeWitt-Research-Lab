    # DRL Web Demonstration and Portfolio Specification

    ## Purpose

    The demonstration proves the most important engineering claims of DRL Web. Every visible result is live, replayed from a signed real trace, cached from a dated artifact, or clearly marked illustrative.

    ## Signature story

    The homepage introduces an independent research laboratory, then lets a visitor open Atticus and follow one integrated workflow across Atlas, FedLens, BalanceLab AI, and EvalForge. The experience resembles a disciplined financial research terminal and tmux workspace while remaining readable, accessible, and truthful.

    ## Modes

    - Static portfolio exploration.
- Persona-specific guided tour.
- Signed execution replay.
- Limited live public Atticus.
- Mobile compact console and desktop multi-pane workspace.

    ## Guided sequence

    - Read the mission and active systems map.
- Choose ordinary exploration or Ask Atticus.
- Open the integrated workflow.
- Watch specialists, evidence, and artifacts in panes.
- Inspect evaluation and a failure entry.
- Continue into project case study, repository, and research.
- Find a contribution issue or founder/résumé context.

    ## Safety and boundedness

    - Live and replay states are labeled.
- Only public and synthetic data.
- No fake metrics or activity.
- No government affiliation implication.
- Accessible motion and muted audio defaults.
- Public tools are bounded and quota-limited.

    ## Measurements shown

    - Run and service status.
- Artifact and source dates.
- Evaluation result.
- System health and maturity.
- Cost and latency when useful.
- Repository/release and contribution links.

    ## Failure and fallback

    - Static content always works.
- GPU cold state offers signed replay.
- Failed live demo explains the failure and degraded path.
- Console always returns to ordinary pages.
- No endless loading or fabricated processing.

    The demo remains keyboard-operable, useful on mobile, compatible with reduced motion, and understandable without sound. A short visual loop, 90-second tour, and technical walkthrough all derive from the same truthful evidence.
