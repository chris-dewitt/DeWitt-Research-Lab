# Open Source Sustainability, Monetization, and Public-Benefit Boundaries

## Principle

DRL intends to sustain and potentially monetize its work without weakening the public modification surface that creates its credibility. Revenue is allowed. Openwashing, artificial crippling, undisclosed sponsor control, and extraction from unpaid contributors are not.

## Preserved revenue paths

DRL may offer:

- managed hosting and operations;
- private or regulated deployments;
- implementation, integration, and consulting;
- paid workshops, courses, and institutional training;
- support and service-level agreements;
- custom evaluation programs;
- private adapters trained on client-owned data;
- sponsored research with independence disclosures;
- grants, donations, and individual sponsorships;
- premium convenience features that do not withhold the core modification surface;
- branded publications, events, and teaching programs.

## What remains part of the commons

For flagship public releases, DRL does not place the following behind a paid tier solely to manufacture dependency:

- core source code and public interfaces;
- public model weights/adapters when redistribution rights permit;
- training and evaluation recipes needed to understand the release;
- public benchmark methodology and scoring code;
- local/self-hosted research profile;
- schemas, CLIs, SDKs, and plugin contracts;
- model, dataset, security, and limitation cards;
- public research results and correction history.

A managed service may be easier, faster, monitored, and supported. It cannot be the only path to exercise the core public capability.

## Sponsor firewall

Sponsors may fund approved work but cannot silently determine benchmark outcomes, suppress unfavorable results, receive undisclosed preferential evaluation, or dictate the general public roadmap. Restricted commissioned work is separated from public research claims. Funding, conflicts, and publication constraints are disclosed.

## Contributor fairness

DRL does not present volunteer labor as staff employment, promise compensation without a written agreement, or claim community artifacts without credit. Commercial use of community contributions follows the repository license and governance. Material contributors are credited in releases; research authorship follows contribution rather than status.

## Brand and trademark

Apache-2.0 code can be commercially reused and forked. DRL's official name, marks, release signatures, and claims of endorsement remain governed by the trademark policy. Forks are welcome; impersonation is not. This distinction permits technical freedom while preserving trust in official releases and paid services.

## Sustainability reporting

The annual Open Research Accountability Report includes:

- public operating cost range and major infrastructure drivers;
- grants, sponsors, and material conflicts;
- paid offerings launched;
- open artifacts released;
- upstream contributions;
- contributor recognition and community health;
- exceptions and restricted artifacts;
- security and maintenance load;
- next-year sustainability risks.

Exact personal finances or confidential client terms need not be disclosed, but the relationship between revenue and research independence must be understandable.
