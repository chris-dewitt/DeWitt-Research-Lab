# Community Code of Conduct

## Our standard

DRL is for serious, curious collaboration. Participants should be respectful, generous with context, precise about evidence, welcoming to learners, and willing to revise conclusions. Critique ideas, methods, code, and claims—not people. Explain jargon and avoid using expertise as a gatekeeping weapon.

Unacceptable behavior includes harassment, threats, discrimination, sexualized attention, doxxing, deliberate humiliation, repeated bad-faith disruption, plagiarism, fabricated results, credential/private-data exposure, or exploiting systems/users outside authorized research boundaries.

## Research and engineering integrity

Participants must disclose material conflicts, preserve provenance, respect licenses and consent, distinguish evidence from inference, report limitations, and correct errors. Security research must follow coordinated disclosure and avoid unnecessary harm. “It was generated by AI” is not an excuse for unreviewed or infringing contribution.

## Enforcement

Report conduct concerns through the private community contact published by DRL. DeWitt or delegated maintainers may warn, restrict, remove content, revoke roles/access, or ban participation, considering severity, safety, pattern, and repair. Confidentiality is respected as far as practical while providing fair process. Retaliation against reporters is prohibited.

## Scope

This applies in DRL repositories, discussions, events, chat spaces, research collaborations, and when someone officially represents the laboratory. It does not create employment or legal obligations beyond applicable policies and law.
