# CFI-005 Synthetic Parameter-Recovery Study

## What this is

The exit evidence for CFI-005 is "tested reference package and synthetic
recovery study." This is the study. It answers one question, asked before any
subject is observed:

> When belief data really were generated by one of the bridge's baselines, does
> the estimator recover the parameters that generated them?

That question has an answer that can be unflattering, which is why it is asked
first. An estimator that cannot recover a parameter from data it generated
itself will not recover it from a person.

**No claim is made here about human or machine belief.** `COMPUTATIONAL_FINANCE
_OF_INTELLIGENCE.md` §2.1 is explicit that the diffusion family is a model
family and a research hypothesis, not an assertion that beliefs follow a
diffusion. This document tests instruments, not subjects. No G2 data was used,
none was needed, and none is implicated.

## Reproduction

```bash
uv run python scripts/run_belief_recovery.py
```

200 replications per model, 400 steps, `dt = 0.05`, horizon 20, seed 20260825.
Every number below is reproducible from that seed on any machine: the simulators
draw from `random.Random(seed)` and the package has no numerical dependencies.

## Results

### Drift-diffusion — recovers well

| Parameter | Truth | Mean estimate | Bias | RMSE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `drift` | +0.4000 | +0.3745 | −0.0255 | 0.1762 |
| `volatility` | +0.8000 | +0.7953 | −0.0047 | 0.0286 |

Volatility recovers to within 0.6%. **The drift interval is wide and that is not
a defect of the estimator.** Drift over a finite horizon has standard error
`sigma / sqrt(T)`; here that is `0.8 / sqrt(20) = 0.179`, and the observed RMSE
is 0.176. The estimator is at the information limit. No method does better on
this much data, and a protocol that needs a tighter drift estimate must buy a
longer horizon rather than a cleverer estimator.

This is recorded because a future reader seeing a drift confidence interval
three times wider than the volatility interval would reasonably suspect a bug.

### Ornstein-Uhlenbeck — level and volatility recover, reversion is biased upward

| Parameter | Truth | Mean estimate | Bias | RMSE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `reversion_rate` | +1.5000 | +1.6610 | **+0.1610** | 0.4898 |
| `level` | +0.7000 | +0.6865 | −0.0135 | 0.0888 |
| `volatility` | +0.6000 | +0.5737 | −0.0263 | 0.0333 |

The reversion rate is biased **upward by 10.7%**. This is the classic
small-sample bias of the autoregressive coefficient, appearing here through the
reparameterisation: least squares attenuates the AR(1) coefficient toward zero,
and `theta = -(rho - 1) / dt` turns that attenuation into an upward push on
`theta`.

It is a known property of the estimator, not a coding error, and it has a
direction: **this estimator will tend to report belief as more strongly
mean-reverting than it is.** Any protocol comparing reversion rates between
cohorts inherits that bias in both arms, but a protocol comparing a fitted
`theta` against a fixed reference value does not, and would need a correction.

The simulator uses the *exact* OU transition law rather than an Euler step, so
this bias is the estimator's alone. Had the simulator also used Euler, it would
have shared the estimator's approximation and the study would have reported a
smaller bias than the estimator actually has.

### Jump-diffusion — the diffusive part recovers, the jump rate does not

| Parameter | Truth | Mean estimate | Bias | RMSE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `drift` | +0.0000 | −0.0030 | −0.0030 | 0.1143 |
| `volatility` | +0.5000 | +0.5124 | +0.0124 | 0.0248 |
| `jump_intensity` | +0.8000 | +0.5288 | **−0.2712** | 0.3111 |

Drift and volatility recover cleanly once jumps are removed. **The jump rate is
under-reported by 34%, and this is a property of threshold detection rather than
a defect to be fixed.**

The mechanism is arithmetic. Increments are flagged as jumps when they exceed
four robust standard deviations, which here is `4 x 0.5 x sqrt(0.05) = 0.447` in
log-odds. With jump sizes drawn from `N(0, 1.2)`, the fraction exceeding that
threshold is `2 x (1 - Phi(0.447 / 1.2)) = 0.709`. The predicted recovered
intensity is therefore `0.8 x 0.709 = 0.567`, against 0.529 observed. The
remaining gap is the undetected jumps inflating the robust scale, which raises
the threshold and hides slightly more.

**A jump smaller than the diffusion's own noise is not detectable, by anything.**
The estimator's obligation is to under-report rather than to guess, and it does.
A protocol needing jump rates must either expect jumps large relative to
diffusion, or sample finely enough that `sqrt(dt)` shrinks the threshold.

## The defect this study found

The first run of this study reported a jump intensity of 0.997 against a truth
of 0.8, with an RMSE of **1.13** — larger than the parameter being estimated.
Fifteen replications in 200 flagged more than 40 jumps; one flagged 155 of 400
increments.

The cause was not the estimator. The simulator was generating belief paths that
left the range the schema can record, and the recorder was **silently clamping**
the reported probability to `1 - 1e-9`. Once a path saturates, every subsequent
record is the same number, every increment is exactly zero, and the robust scale
collapses — in the worst replication to 0.046 against a true 0.5, so ordinary
diffusive movement was flagged as jumps.

The design parameters made this inevitable: drift 0.3 over horizon 20 plus 16
expected jumps of +1.2 reaches log-odds ≈ 25, and the schema tops out at
**±20.723**, which is `p = 0.999999999`.

Two corrections followed, and the second is the one that matters:

1. The recovery design now uses zero-mean jumps, which is also the more natural
   model of belief revision — revisions go both ways.
2. **Saturation is now an error.** `SaturatedBeliefError` names the bound and
   the step at which the path crossed it. A belief the instrument cannot
   represent must not be silently converted into one it can.

The second correction is a standing property of the package, not a fix to this
study. It is the same failure this laboratory recorded in `EVAL-0001` earlier the
same day from the other direction: an instrument that quietly produces a
plausible number instead of refusing.

## Limitations and non-claims

- Three model families at one design point each. Recovery at other parameter
  values, horizons, and sampling rates is unmeasured. In particular the OU bias
  grows with `dt` and the jump miss-rate depends on the jump-to-diffusion size
  ratio; neither was swept.
- Exact and asymmetric Bayesian updating are tested for correctness in
  `tests/cfi/test_baselines.py` but are not in this study, because with additive
  log-odds updating and no reporting noise they recover exactly and a Monte
  Carlo study over them measures nothing.
- 200 replications fixes the Monte Carlo error on each bias at roughly
  `spread / sqrt(200)`. Biases smaller than that are not resolved by this study.
- **No thresholds are asserted anywhere.** Whether a 10.7% upward bias in
  reversion rate is acceptable depends on an estimand and a tolerance that a
  preregistered protocol fixes. G3 has not been passed. This document reports and
  stops.
- Nothing here bears on whether beliefs are diffusions. It bears only on whether
  these estimators work when they are.
