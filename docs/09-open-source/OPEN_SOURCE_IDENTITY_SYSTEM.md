# Open Source Institutional Identity System

## Identity thesis

DeWitt Research Workshop presents open technology as a living research tradition rather than a software-distribution tactic. The laboratory's personality combines an independent academic institute, a financial research workstation, a public technical archive, and a late-night systems laboratory. The cream-on-black visual system communicates seriousness; the open artifact system communicates trust.

The primary institutional line is:

> **Intelligence for Good. Intelligence for All.**

The operating proof beneath that line is:

> **Open models. Open software. Public evaluation. Local operation. Reproducible research.**

Neither statement may stand alone. Public-interest language without inspectable artifacts is marketing; artifacts without a humane mission are merely inventory.

## Narrative hierarchy

1. **Laboratory:** DeWitt Research Workshop is an independent research initiative founded and directed by Christopher Noxon DeWitt.
2. **Mission:** useful intelligence should be understandable, modifiable, and accessible beyond large institutions.
3. **Atticus:** Atticus operates the laboratory and is itself a public research program in open agent models, orchestration, permissions, and local-first computing.
4. **Specialists:** Atlas, FedLens, BalanceLab AI, and EvalForge are inspectable specialist systems and research instruments.
5. **Commons:** software, model artifacts, datasets, benchmark methods, schemas, teaching material, and failure evidence are released for use and improvement.
6. **Community:** collaborators are credited participants in the research program, not unpaid background labor.
7. **Upstream:** DRL visibly acknowledges and contributes to the projects that make its work possible.

## Public identity vocabulary

Preferred labels:

- Open Research Laboratory
- Atticus Open Model Commons
- Open Stack
- Public Evaluation Archive
- Reproduction Bundle
- Local Research Profile
- Community Replication
- Upstream Contribution
- Experimental / Alpha / Beta / Stable
- Open-source software / open-weight model / source-available artifact, used precisely

Avoid:

- "proprietary magic" or mystery language that conceals mechanism;
- "open-source model" for a model that only exposes weights under custom terms;
- "community-driven" before the community has actual influence and recognition;
- "decentralized" when DRL remains centrally governed;
- "free forever" for services with real operating costs;
- "production ready" without published operational evidence.

## Website behaviors

Open identity appears through behavior, not decoration:

- every flagship page has **Run**, **Read**, **Reproduce**, **Fork**, and **Contribute** paths;
- model responses can expose model identity, digest, runtime, tool trace, and evaluation status;
- every metric links to methodology and artifact version;
- every architecture diagram identifies open components and managed boundaries;
- every project shows upstream lineage and DRL modifications;
- every artifact card states license, maturity, hardware, cost assumptions, and last verification date;
- closed or restricted elements display a plain-language exception rather than disappearing.

## Institutional rituals

DRL maintains recurring public rituals that reinforce the identity:

- monthly open artifact release notes;
- quarterly replication and failure review;
- upstream contribution digest;
- public research office hours or recorded seminar;
- annual Open Research Accountability Report;
- contributor roll call and research acknowledgments;
- periodic clean-room installation day;
- Atticus model bakeoff and benchmark publication.

## Guardrails against openwashing

A public repository does not qualify a project for the Open Source portal unless it has a license, preferred modification source, runnable setup, validation evidence, maintenance status, and named owner. A downloadable model does not receive an Open Source AI badge solely because weights are available. A benchmark does not receive a reproducible badge if scoring code or required data is unavailable. A community contribution is not claimed as DRL work without attribution.

## Identity acceptance tests

A representative student, open-source maintainer, researcher, and hiring manager must each be able to explain DRL's open model and software commitments after a five-minute site walkthrough. At least one participant should complete a local quickstart without private assistance. The test report records confusion, broken expectations, and terminology errors.
