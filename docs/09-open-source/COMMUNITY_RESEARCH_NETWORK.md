# DRL Community Research Network, Mentorship, and Recognition

## Purpose

DRL's target audience includes collaborators, tinkerers, students, academics, learners, and teachers. The community program should let people enter at different levels and make work that matters, without requiring them to already be professional machine-learning engineers.

## Participation paths

- **Explorer:** runs demos, reads papers, reports reproduction results, and joins discussions.
- **Learner contributor:** improves docs, examples, tests, translations, accessibility, or fixtures.
- **Engineering contributor:** implements connectors, adapters, evaluators, tools, UI, or infrastructure.
- **Data contributor:** creates reviewed tasks, annotations, source metadata, or benchmark slices.
- **Research contributor:** proposes experiments, reproduces results, analyzes failures, or coauthors reports.
- **Teacher:** adapts DRL labs for classes, creates assignments, or reports classroom outcomes.
- **Maintainer/researcher:** accepts sustained responsibility under DRL governance.

No path is treated as lesser. Documentation, evaluation, accessibility, review, and teaching are first-class contributions.

## Mentorship system

Each mentored issue identifies:

- expected background;
- learning objectives;
- estimated scope rather than a false time promise;
- mentor or review owner;
- required reading;
- setup command;
- acceptance evidence;
- stretch research questions;
- safe boundaries and data/license constraints.

DRL should hold periodic public office hours or recorded architecture clinics when capacity permits. The schedule must not promise support the laboratory cannot sustain.

## Research sprints

A sprint is a bounded public program around one question, for example:

- improve Atticus permission-boundary cases;
- reproduce Core/Edge quantization results;
- add an Atlas public connector;
- audit FedLens citation spans;
- create BalanceLab property tests;
- compare open model tool parsers;
- improve screen-reader use of the Atticus console.

Each sprint publishes the question, starting evidence, issue set, data rules, evaluation method, and closing report. Sponsors may fund a sprint but cannot purchase a result.

## Recognition

DRL recognizes contributions through:

- release notes and contributor records;
- model and dataset cards;
- paper acknowledgments or authorship under scholarly criteria;
- website contributor profiles, with consent;
- maintainer and DRL researcher designations under governance;
- certificates or badges for completed teaching/research programs when they describe evidence accurately;
- invited talks, demos, or seminar presentations.

Commit counts do not determine status. Review judgment, care for users, provenance, reproducibility, and sustained responsibility matter more.

## Contributor safety and inclusion

The community program must protect:

- beginners from vague “good first issues” that secretly require architectural expertise;
- contributors from pressure to donate private data;
- researchers from undisclosed sponsor influence;
- maintainers from unlimited support expectations;
- users from unsafe plugins or unreviewed claims;
- contributors' right to receive attribution and correction.

The Code of Conduct, security policy, data policy, and governance apply across GitHub, Hugging Face, seminars, chat, and events.

## Student and academic use

DRL encourages coursework and independent studies built around public artifacts. The website should provide citation formats, stable release snapshots, DOI links where available, instructor notes, and small-compute alternatives. Student work can be listed as an external replication or community extension only with permission and clear attribution.

## Community health measures

Useful measures include:

- first-contribution success rate;
- median review age and abandoned PR count;
- documentation/setup failure reports;
- diversity of contribution type;
- issue clarity scores from new contributors;
- maintainer load;
- external replication count;
- percentage of releases with non-code contributors recognized;
- unresolved conduct or attribution concerns.

These metrics are interpreted carefully and are not public leaderboards of individuals.
